Two reasons could explain why, and they sound alike.

The first is what the other person expects: it hurts you to fall short of it. Charness and
Dufwenberg (2006) and Battigalli and Dufwenberg (2007) call this guilt aversion.

The second is your word: it hurts you to break it, whatever the other person expects.

Usually the two come together, because the other expects something of you precisely because you
promised it. Telling them apart takes a case where the expectation stays and the promise does not.
Vanberg (2008) built one by switching partners. In his framing, each reason predicts something
different:

| Reason                 | Same partner | You get a new partner                                                                |
| ---------------------- | ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| What the other expects | you roll     | you roll just the same: the other expects the same and doesn't know about the switch |
| Your word              | you roll     | you roll less: you promised this person nothing                                      |
