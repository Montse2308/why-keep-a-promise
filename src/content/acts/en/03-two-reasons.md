There are two reasons to keep a promise that no longer pays, and they sound alike.

The first is not letting the other person down: it hurts you to fall short of what they expect of
you. Charness and Dufwenberg (2006) and Battigalli and Dufwenberg (2007) call this guilt aversion.

The second is having given your word: it hurts you to break it, whatever the other person expects.

In daily life the two come together: the other person expects something of you because you
promised it. To tell them apart you need a case where the expectation stays and your promise does
not. Vanberg (2008) built one by switching partners. In his framing, each reason predicts something
different:

| Reason                 | Same partner | You get a new partner                                                                |
| ---------------------- | ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| What the other expects | you roll     | you roll just the same: the other expects the same and doesn't know about the switch |
| Your word              | you roll     | you roll less: you promised this person nothing                                      |
