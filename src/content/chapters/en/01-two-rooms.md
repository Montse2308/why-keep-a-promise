### Before the promise, a famous game: the prisoner's dilemma.

Two people in separate rooms. Each chooses to cooperate or to defect, without knowing what the
other does and without being able to talk.

<!-- beat:play -->

### Play once.

If you both cooperate, you each get {R} coins. If you both defect, {P} each. If only one of you
defects, that one takes {T} and the other gets {S}.

<!-- beat:columns -->

### Why defect? Think like the other.

Take the other's two moves one at a time, and see what pays you more against each.

<!-- beat:trap -->

### Whatever you do, defecting pays more.

And the other reasons just as you do. So both defect and end up with {P} each, when cooperating
would have given them {R}.

What if they played again and again? No one tells it better than
[*The Evolution of Trust*](https://ncase.me/trust/), by Case (2017).
